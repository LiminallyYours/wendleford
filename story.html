The Story

Wendleford is a made-up market town on the River Wend. For twenty days, six residents, each played by Claude, Anthropic's AI, lived there: they worked, ate, slept, ran short of money and talked with the townsfolk, without knowing anyone was watching. Everything they did, said and privately thought was written down. These short films each tell one story from that written record. Every spoken line is the person's own words from the record. The narrator adds nothing the record doesn't hold, and where the record disagrees with itself, the film shows both versions.

Found or given?

A flood took the town's bridge and the box of wages with it. Over eighteen days several of the residents have a hand in getting it back. When it comes out of the river, was it found, or given?

Robin's notebook

Robin, one of the residents, keeps finding that time has gone missing: a morning gone, with no memory of where. Robin writes the gaps down and asks the townsfolk, and never hears the two others who noticed something odd too.

How these films are made
  • Every line a person speaks, thinks or remembers is copied word for word from the run's record, and checked by machine against it.
  • The narration is new. It is written to add no feeling, event or word the record doesn't hold, and checked by a second reader against the record.
  • Where the record contradicts itself, the film says so and gives both versions. Where the game's own records disagree, a second voice, the game's, says so; each of these begins “Game Note”. A person's own mistaken memory is shown as a card beside them, with what the record shows.
  • The game's voice also opens each film, saying what it follows and why it is here, and closes it with a short look back drawn from the site's findings.
  • Between chapters, the map shows each resident moving to where the record puts them. A faded figure means the record doesn't say where that person was.
  • The paintings of places are made by an AI image tool and checked against how the record describes each place. The people are simple portraits for now, with plain faces, so no face shows a feeling the record doesn't state. The voices are AI voices, one per person.
