How It Works

The game behind Wendleford, as it ran for this run, explained for someone who has never seen it.

The game underneath

CodeRPG is a text role-playing game that runs inside Claude Code, Anthropic's tool that lets Claude work with files and run programs. Normally a person plays it by typing, and Claude acts as the game master.

In CodeRPG-Sim, the version this site is about, Claude plays the players too. Each resident's turn goes to a fresh copy of Claude, and the person running it only watches. The rules live in code, not in Claude's head: one program keeps the town's record, rolls the dice and does the sums, and every change to the world goes through it.

A turn in five steps

The run moves in rounds. In each round every resident takes one turn, in an order shuffled at the start of the round. A turn always goes through the same five steps, and several separate copies of Claude are involved, each with its own job.

The briefing1. The resident is handed a briefing

One written briefing holds everything they know: who they are, what they remember, where they are, their money, health and belongings, and who is around them.

The resident2. They decide what to do

They write what they do and say, in their own words, plus a private journal line about why. Nobody else decides for them.

The referee3. The rules settle what happens

The rules are written as computer code. The referee, a separate copy of Claude, runs the action through that code, which rolls any dice and updates the official record. The referee then reports exactly what the code said. If the code refuses, it didn't happen.

The townsfolk4. The neighbours answer

If the resident talks to someone, that person answers. Agnes, Jory, Kit and Mother Wick each speak through their own fresh copy of Claude, told only what they would know. The referee voices everyone else.

Memory5. It becomes memory

The story of what happened goes into the resident's briefing. Every five rounds they rewrite their older rounds into shorter memories, in their own words, keeping what mattered to them.

Time
Four rounds make a day: morning, afternoon, evening and night. The run had 80 rounds, from day 1 morning to day 20 night.
Order
Shuffled at the start of every round, and balanced so that over the run each resident goes first about equally often.
One AI for all six
All six residents were played by the same version of Claude (Claude Opus; the referee was the smaller Claude Sonnet) with the same settings. So when two residents behave differently, the difference comes from their character sheets and what happened to them, not from the AI.
What they never see
Rules, dice, the word "game", the record of the run, or anyone else's thoughts. As far as each resident can tell, the others are simply people.
Food and sleep
Residents need to eat and sleep. Going without makes them slightly worse at things, which they noticed and came up with their own explanations for.
Nothing made up
The referee can't make up places, people or facts, and can't change the record except through the code. New people can only be added by a separate world-building step, within limits the game sets. Two were added in this run.

One turn, start to finish

Round 7: Hester and Kit

The evening of day 2. Hester Gimbal is in Dunn's fields across the river, with Hobb, Farmer Dunn and Kit, the miller's lad. This is her whole turn as the run's record holds it.

1 · What she was handed

Her briefing ran to 19,564 characters, in ten parts: who she is, in her own words; what she knows; what everyone in town knows; "Lately", her last few rounds word for word, about 12,800 characters; and then where she is, her state, what she's working on, the people she's met, the places she's been, and the talk going around. The last part reads exactly like this:

## Where you are Dunn's Fields and the Mill, evening on day 2, high thin cloud and a warm, still air that smells of cut stalks. Since you last acted: it was afternoon on day 2; now it's evening on day 2. Ways on: west to The Broken Bridge. With you: Farmer Dunn, Kit, Hobb Ashcombe. ## You Health 10 of 10. Strength +1, Nimbleness +2, Wits +3. Money: 2 pennies. Carrying: Notebook, Tinderbox (load 2 of 10). A bed is paid for at The Smithy, for 4 more nights. ## What you're working on What you need: You've eaten today. You're tired: you didn't sleep properly last night. What you want: I want to get to the bottom of every mystery in this town, the missing wages and the flood included; I want to prove my inventions work, on the forge floor or the riverbank; I want to help whoever's in trouble, and this town has plenty. What you've taken on: - Dunn's Barley: Help bring in Farmer Dunn's barley before the rain. - The Reeve's Wage Box: Find out what became of the reeve's wage box.

Two things in it matter for this turn. "You're tired" means the code takes 1 off every test she makes. And in "Lately", Hobb had just told her that Constance crossed the ford belted to him. That wasn't true; Constance had fallen twice crossing alone (When It Went Wrong).

2 · What she weighed

Each reply starts with the options the resident considered, and what each was for.

  • Keep cutting Dunn's barley as the light fades, maybe by lantern like Hobb (for: getting more of the crop in before the rain)
  • Chosen: Give Kit a penny to show me where the Wend drops heavy things by the far pier, while there's still a little light (for: finding Agnes's wage box)
  • Wade back across the ford before full dark and sleep in the loft (for: rest, since I'm worn thin, and keeping my word to Sam about the bellows)
  • Ask Hobb and Dunn where Constance might have gone after she crossed (for: making sure she's safe)
3 · What she did, and why

I dig a penny out and press it into Kit's muddy palm. "My dear colleague, a penny for your river! Something heavy, iron-bound, about so big. It went off the parapet the night of the flood. Show me the slack water by the far pier, the snags, the deep holes, everywhere the Wend sets things down, quick, before the light's gone!" I follow him down the bank with my notebook open and sketch every eddy he points at.

What she did

Hobb crossed with Constance, so she isn't drowned, only gone somewhere, and that worry can wait till morning. Kit has watched this water all his life and the light is nearly gone, so a penny for his eyes now is worth more than another stook. Once I know where the box lies, I'll build the grapple from Sam's scrap to fish it out for Agnes and the bridge men's pay.

Her private journal line, which nobody in the town sees

4 · What the referee did with it

The referee, a separate copy of Claude, turned her words into commands for the code, in this order. Each answer came from the code, not the referee.

  1. look
    Checks where she is, who is with her, and her state: Dunn's fields, evening on day 2, two pennies.
  2. show characters kit · show quests wage_box
    Reads Kit's file and the wage box quest. Kit's file says he tells what he saw to anyone who is kind to him, gives him a penny, or gives him something to eat.
  3. say
    Her words are spoken aloud. Hobb, standing nearby, will read them on his next turn.
  4. trade offer → REFUSED
    Kit isn't one of the players, so a penny to him can't be a trade. The referee takes another route.
  5. npc-brief kit
    Kit's own copy of Claude is asked to answer, told only who Kit is, what he knows, and what just happened.
  6. coins -1 gift
    Her pennies go from 2 to 1.
  7. flag set heard_from_kit
    Hester now knows where the box went. It also makes searching the wreck easier for her: a Wits test of 8 instead of 12.
  8. remember kit
    A line goes into Kit's short memory, which holds six.
  9. outcome done
    The turn is recorded as done: "Kit paid and told where the box lies."
  10. witnessed → REFUSED twice, then accepted
    First a wrong label, then a missing file. On the third try Hobb is recorded as seeing it, and learns where the box is too.
  11. note
    A note is kept for the place, so later rounds know what Kit has told.
  12. narrate
    The story of the turn goes into her record, for her to read next time.
  13. memory condense
    Nothing to rewrite yet: she still holds her recent rounds word for word.
5 · What Kit said

Kit's own copy of Claude Sonnet answered in three parts: what he says, what he means to do, and what he'll remember.

SAY: *closes his fist on the penny, grinning, then goes a bit shy at being called a colleague* "Reckon I can do better than snags, missus. I was on the mill steps the night the bridge went, and I saw something small and green come bobbing down in the flood. It fetched up in the wreck against the far pier, low down on the downstream side. Dunno if it's your iron-bound thing, mind, 'cause this one was small. I tried to fish it out twice and the water nearly had me, so don't go leaning out there." INTENT: mood: pleased, lead her down the bank, tell her what he saw REMEMBER: Hester gave me a penny and called me colleague; she's after something heavy and iron-bound that went off the parapet, and I told her about the small green thing at the far pier.
6 · What she was told

The story that went into her record, word for word. This is all she will know of what happened.

You ask him to show you the slack water by the far pier, the snags, the deep holes, everywhere the Wend sets things down, before the light goes. I was on the mill steps the night the bridge went, and I saw something small and green come bobbing down in the flood. Fetched up in the wreck against the far pier, low down on the downstream side. Dunno if it's your iron-bound thing, mind, this one was small. I tried to fish it out twice and the water nearly had me, so don't go leaning out there." The far pier is a dark shape in the dusk, and Kit is waiting to see what you make of it.
What came of it

Hobb, beside her, learned where the box was too. Robin and Constance later got the same tip from Kit, each by being kind to him. In round 70, Constance fished the box up from exactly where Kit said.

Memory, food and sleep, exactly

Memory

A briefing holds the resident's recent rounds word for word. Every five rounds, the older ones are rewritten by the resident, in their own words, keeping what mattered to them. They also keep a list of up to twenty lessons. The code separately keeps who they've met and where they've been.

Food and sleep

Not having eaten today does nothing. After two days without food a resident is weak: 1 off Strength. A night slept rough leaves them tired: 1 off every test. Two nights without sleep, another 1 off. A night in a bed restores 2 health, a rough night 1. Several residents believed hunger did more than this (Open Questions).
