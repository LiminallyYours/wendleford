When It Went Wrong

The places where the game itself made mistakes, and what the residents came to believe because of them. The errors are part of what happened: the residents lived with them, explained them to themselves, and sometimes built their days around them.

Mistakes that shaped beliefs

The supper that could never happen

What happened
Farmer Dunn promised a harvest supper to everyone who helped. The barley came in on the evening of day 3, the supper never came, and days later Dunn himself still said "What's owed now is the supper." "Dunn owes a harvest supper" stayed among Constance's, Hester's and Hobb's lessons into the last days of the run.

Behind it
The town's files only call the supper if the barley is in by the morning of day 3. Coming in that evening was too late, by the files' own rule.

Lines that went stale

What happened
Jory told Robin the bridge wasn't done while standing on its finished timbers. Agnes told Robin, Hobb and Dilly in turn that "the town owes you" after paying them in full, and told Robin "the bridge wants backs" after it was mended. Robin wrote each one down as evidence. Hobb refused the debt: "It's square."

Behind it
Some townsfolk lines are shown every time, and were written for the town as it was before the bridge was mended. Nothing updated them.

A belief from a refused action

What happened
For 30 rounds Constance believed that stout staffs could be cut from the willows by the ford. It led the group to buy a five-penny hatchet that was never used. In the end Jory sawed the pole from ash.

Behind it
In round 6 she tried to cut a staff, and the rules refused it: the town has no such thing. The belief came out of that attempt all the same.

Stuck at the smithy

What happened
In rounds 62 to 66, Constance, Hester and Dilly stood at Sam's anvil trying to pay four pennies together for the hooked pole. Constance said they had "been stood about this anvil all night". In the end she took the others' pennies into her own hand and paid.

Behind it
The game lets only one person pay a price. Constance had no pennies, Hester two and Dilly three, so the payment was refused three times while all three described pooling their pennies.

A plan taken as done

What happened
In round 70 Hester asked Agnes whether the box was "all there" before anyone had found it. Agnes corrected her, and Constance fished it out the same round.

Behind it
Hester's briefing described the plan to fish the box out, and she read it as already done.

Answers that never arrived

What happened
Robin asked Hester whether time ever jumps for her, and Marigold whether her offers to mend Robin's cuff feel new each time. Hester answered "like a door opening!" in round 45; Marigold told Hester "Goodness, they do!" in round 75. Robin heard neither.

Behind it
A resident hears only what is said where they are, while they are there. Hester answered after Robin had walked off, and Marigold answered to Hester, a round later.

Seams Robin noticed

How the game works, showing through

Not all of these are mistakes. Some are simply how the game works, seen from the inside. Most residents took them in their stride; Robin, whose sheet says they suspect they're an AI, wrote them down.

Time that goes missing

Every briefing opens by saying how much time has passed since the resident last acted: "it was morning; now it's afternoon." Each round moves the clock a quarter of a day. Robin read it as lost hours.

Townsfolk who forget

Wat Birch forgot an agreement he had made with Robin to cross the ford together. Townsfolk keep only short memories. Robin wrote both versions down side by side.

Offers made as if for the first time

Marigold kept offering to mend Robin's cuff as if she never had before. Her rewritten memories had dropped the earlier offers. Asked whether they felt new each time, she said they did.

Robin's Notebook follows every piece of Robin's evidence, with what was behind it.

Memories that went wrong

Every five rounds, each resident rewrites their older rounds as a shorter memory in their own words. Some of what they wrote never happened. Hobb told Hester that Constance had crossed the ford belted to him, when she had fallen twice crossing alone. Hester remembered being paid back handsomely for half a loaf she had in fact turned down. Dilly remembered holding the pole when the box came up, though at the end of Dilly's own turn the pole was in Constance's hands. A reading of the record suggests these errors lean towards what each character sheet wants, which is a hint to test, not a finding.

Smaller slips

  • The referee misread Hobb's "off to the loft" in round 23 and charged him two pennies for Sam's loft instead of the free hayloft. Hobb's later memory called it his own choice.
  • The pole was once placed at Jory's gatepost after Constance had already carried it to the smithy (round 63).
  • One turn's result was lost (Hester, round 51), and two notes the referee meant to keep for later rounds were never stored (rounds 64 and 69).
  • Over the run the code refused 102 commands. That is the check working: a refused command didn't happen, so the record stays true.

A town that ran dry

Once the harvest, the bridge and the box were done, paid work thinned out. Townsfolk carry no money, so pennies only come into the town as wages and leave it on bread and beds. Only two newcomers arrived all run, nobody pinned a notice to the board, and no side-quest was ever made. The residents invented work instead: gates and roofs for Widow Mabry, stakes for the ford.

What comes of it

Most of these come from how today's game is built: lines written once, payments only one person can make, events tied to one exact morning, short memories for townsfolk. They are kept in the record and studied rather than quietly fixed after the fact, because each one shaped what the residents believed. They are part of why the game is being redesigned; Where It's Heading describes the plans.

Source: one 80-round run of the CodeRPG-Sim simulation (filed as run "step76-30"): its record, a reading of it afterwards, and the interviews that followed, 29 to 30 October 2026. One run is a single sample, so treat every hint here as worth testing again, not as a finding.
