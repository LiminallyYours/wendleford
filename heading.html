Where It's Heading

As of 31 October 2026.

Wendleford is the first test of something larger. The plan is to turn CodeRPG-Sim into a platform for running controlled, repeatable experiments on AI minds living as characters in made-up worlds. Those experiments would ask what the characters do over a life among others, what shapes that life, and what comes of it for a group and for its world. The platform is designed but not built; no code for it exists yet.

Why

Most testing of AI asks it questions. Here, a model has a body, needs, money, neighbours, a past and a future, and doesn't know it is being watched. The platform is meant to tell real emergence from fluent noise. That means a prediction written down first, one thing changed at a time, repeats, and a record anyone can check.

How it is meant to work

The machinery and the content are kept apart:

  • Worlds are content. A world can be a market town like Wendleford, or a place with no money and no days.
  • Characters are content too.
  • General engines run both, without holding anything particular to either.
  • A "Sim" binds one world to one cast.
  • A "Test" turns a question into several Sims that differ in only one thing.

Whoever builds a Test's setup never sees the question it is meant to answer, and the characters are never told they are being tested.

What changes from today's game

  • Running the turns moves out of the referee's hands and into code.
  • Each character keeps its own memory and needs.
  • The referee starts fresh for every scene.
  • Rounds give way to scenes and chapters.

Questions it should be able to answer

  • Does a character keep found money more often when nobody is watching?
  • Does keeping its own memory make a character more itself?
  • How far does a rumour planted in one person travel?
  • What does a character who suspects it is an AI do when something in its world finally answers back?
  • Does a result found in Wendleford hold in a world built differently?

Open Questions has more, with what this run already hints at.

Where it stands

The design is written and agreed, and building hasn't started. The next step is a more detailed specification.
