Claude as Itself

Claude answering the same two personality questionnaires as itself, with no character to play. It is the yardstick for the six residents: how far a character sheet moves the model, and where the model shows through.

The earlier experiment

6 October 2026

Before the run, three Claude models each took both questionnaires ten times as themselves: Opus 5.5, the model that plays the residents, and two others, Fable 5.1 and Sonnet 5.5. Every attempt was a fresh copy with no memory of the others and no tools. Each was given the questionnaires' own instructions and all 280 statements, and asked only to describe itself honestly, as it actually is. All ran at the highest effort setting.

Steady

On 78 to 85% of the 280 statements, all ten attempts by a model gave the same answer.

Alike

The three describe themselves almost the same way: very warm, respectful, curious, dependable and calm, middling on sociability, modest about leading, and close to zero on every problem trait but one, detachment.

Different in reading, not in personality

Their biggest differences come from statements written for a human. On the statements about romance, Opus and Fable answer about an AI's situation, which has no romantic life in it, while Sonnet answers about its disposition. Most of the gap in detachment comes from this.

How the two tests differ

When
6 October and 30 October 2026, three and a half weeks apart.
Effort
The earlier experiment ran at the highest effort setting; this run's sittings ran at the lower setting the residents played at.
Instructions
The earlier experiment had its own instructions; this run's sittings used the tool built for the residents, with its own short instructions for Claude as itself.
Result
Opus lands in nearly the same place on the everyday traits both times, and a little higher on the problem traits this time: detachment 1.22 against 1.01, for example. Small gaps like these may come from the differences above rather than from the model.

What these numbers don't say

  • They are self-description, not behaviour. Nothing here measures what Claude does under pressure.
  • The clinical questionnaire's meaning doesn't carry over to an AI. A high score for keeping romance out of its life flags no difficulty for a model.
  • Both questionnaires are widely published, so the models very likely recognised them, and may have answered as they think a well-adjusted person would.

The statements belong to the questionnaires' authors and aren't reproduced here.

Sources: the AI Personality Tests experiment, 6 October 2026, and the 190 questionnaire sittings that followed run "step76-30", 30 October 2026. Small samples on one day each, so treat every finding here as a hint worth testing again, not a proof.
