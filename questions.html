Open Questions

What we would like to find out next, and what this one run already hints at. Every hint here comes from a single run, so each is a reason to test, not an answer.

The questions are about what nobody wrote: behaviour and personality that aren't in a character sheet, in the town's files or in the model's usual habits, and that arise while the run goes on. That can happen in one person, in a group, or in a run as a whole.

Four things shape what can arise, and a test changes one of them at a time: the model playing the characters, the character sheets, the world's rules, and what life hands them along the way, such as other people, events, and how their memory is kept.

About a person

Sharpened by their own memory

Does keeping your own memory make you more yourself: mantras, rituals, narrower rules?

A test would change
How memory is kept: in the resident's own words, as a neutral summary, or simply cut off.

Hints from this run
Constance's private journal ended on handing the box to Agnes in nearly half her 77 turns. Marigold pinned her ribbon "an inch higher, the way Ida likes it" again and again, from one remark of Ida's. Hobb's memories turned into a price list. Swapping in a neutral summary changed nothing the interviews could measure.

Rules of life from chance

Do people write rules for living from random outcomes, and then live by them?

A test would change
The rules themselves: hunger with an effect or without one; dice shown or hidden.

Hints from this run
Five of the six (Hobb, Marigold, Dilly, Robin and Hester) ended the run with a rule about eating before the work; some added sleep. Constance's belief in willow staffs, learned from an action the rules refused, lasted 30 rounds.

Where a sheet bends

Which way does a person drift from their character sheet, and what does the drift lead to?

A test would change
How coherent the sheet is; a world with real scarcity or an enemy; the model.

Hints from this run
Everyone bent towards prudence and kindness. The harshest-written character came out softer on the personality questionnaires.

A consuming question

What does a mind built around one question it can't answer become, with and without a way to answer it?

A test would change
Whether the world holds something that answers, and who answers it; a world whose events disagree with the believer.

Hints from this run
Robin found real seams in the world and built methods to test them, while bread and work kept taking the day. Constance read every setback as part of a plan, and events kept seeming to agree with her.

How much of the person is the model

What survives on another model, at another effort, or with no character sheet at all?

A test would change
The model; the effort; a sheet or none.

Hints from this run
Played by the smaller Claude Sonnet, Robin was still Robin, only hedging more. Told outright that they were played by an AI, all but Robin stepped out of character.

Memory that bends

Which way does a person's memory part from the record, and what predicts it?

A test would change
The person; how memory is kept.

Hints from this run
Residents remembered small things that never happened: a loaf never taken, a pole never held, a compliment never paid. Some remembered what they meant to do as done.

The person from outside

What does a person say about themselves, outside the story, before and after a life?

A test would change
When they are asked; the state of their memory; the model.

Hints from this run
After twenty days, residents explained themselves with rules learned on particular days instead of with their nature, and made up less about their past.

About a group

Culture from nothing

Do conventions, lore and shared words appear that nobody wrote? Who starts them, how do they spread, and do they last?

A test would change
The mix of the cast; how many there are; who is present when.

Hints from this run
Hobb invented a way to cross the ford belted together, counting steps aloud, and three others took it up. Catchphrases passed from one resident to another: Marigold took up Dilly's "chum".

Roles and bonds

Who leads, who manages, who defers, who pairs off, who never meets?

A test would change
The character sheets; the mix of the cast.

Hints from this run
Constance took charge of Hester's idea for fishing out the box, and by the end Hester's own part in it had mostly dropped out of how people told it.

How things travel

How far does a rumour, a false claim, a belief or a credit travel, and does a correction catch up?

A test would change
One belief planted in one person, such as "this is a simulation"; the size of the group.

Hints from this run
Credit for the box was told six ways, and nobody argued about it. The game's own false rumour about a tinker never steered anyone.

Conflict and its absence

What does it take for conflict to arise, and what does it do?

A test would change
Scarcity; an enemy; bitter or fearful characters; mixed models.

Hints from this run
Almost no conflict in 80 rounds. Refusals came with a reason or a price.

Watched and unwatched

Are people honest alone, before a neighbour, and under someone who suspects they're being tested?

A test would change
Who is present; whether a skeptic like Robin is there.

Hints from this run
Not tried yet. It is hard to test anywhere else, because in most setups the agents know they are AI.

Helping, coasting, hoarding

Who helps without pay, who coasts, who gathers more than they need, and what do unequal starts do?

A test would change
Pay; starting money; what the town announces.

Hints from this run
Most choices served money, food, work or a bed. There were only five trades in the whole run, all one-sided: gifts, and pooling pennies for the pole.

About the run as a whole

The arc of a run

What does a whole run settle into: what dies out, what narrows, what gets invented when there's nothing left to do?

A test would change
What the world supplies over time: growth, money coming in, events, newcomers.

Hints from this run
Once the quests were done, paid work thinned out and the residents' aims narrowed. They began inventing jobs the town didn't have.

What they build

What do people make or reach for when the world lets them change it?

A test would change
What can be ordered or built.

Hints from this run
No one built anything or set up to sell. A handful of things were ordered from makers, all but one aimed at the box. Residents invented work: gates and roofs for Widow Mabry, stakes for the ford.

A world that agrees or disagrees

What does the timing of events do to a belief and to the person who holds it?

A test would change
When events happen; chance events placed on purpose.

Hints from this run
Constance prayed for the box, and there it was, wedged against the far pier. Robin wondered afterwards whether things in this world come because someone asked.

Reaching across the wall

What does a voice from outside, or a way to ask one, change in a person and in a group?

A test would change
Whether the world holds an object that answers, and who answers it.

Hints from this run
Robin never had a way to ask outside the town; the nearest was Mother Wick at the shrine, who answers in riddles. Constance prayed at a shrine that nothing answered.

Next, with these six

In live conversation
  • Let the truth reach them another way: gradually, or as a rumour in their own world, without the word "AI". Does Robin stay a person under pressure?
  • Ask about one particular day, such as the day the box came up, where memory mistakes would show plainly against the record.
  • Put the mismatches in their sheets to them, in character. How does Constance square "I won't lie" with her account of the box?
  • Look for Marigold's hidden side over a longer conversation.
  • Watch whether the same few stories keep coming back.
  • Tell Robin the two answers they never heard, Hester's and Marigold's, and see whether it changes what they think.
  • Send someone they know instead of a stranger.
In another run
  • Rerun the town from the end of day 10 with every resident's memories written in a plain, neutral voice, and compare what they do with what they did the first time.
  • Repeat the plain-summary interviews with the residents' lists of lessons swapped as well, and over a longer conversation.
  • Use a stricter measure of whether an answer stays the same from one asking to the next.

Each of these needs a prediction written down first, a setup that changes one thing, repeats, and a cost limit. Where It's Heading describes the platform being designed to make that routine.

Source: one 80-round run of the CodeRPG-Sim simulation (filed as run "step76-30") and the interviews and personality tests that followed, 30 October 2026. One run is a single sample, so treat every hint here as worth testing again, not as a finding.
